# Counterfactual Newsroom

Turn one changed premise into a sequence of believable newspaper editions. Treat the result as
disciplined speculative analysis presented through journalism, not as disconnected fiction.

## Establish the divergence dossier

Before writing articles, determine:

1. the real-world baseline immediately before the divergence;
2. the single changed premise and the date it begins;
3. what remains unchanged at first;
4. the actors, incentives, resources, and institutions through which effects can spread;
5. the largest uncertainty in the premise.

If the request omits publication years, choose at least three dates that reveal immediate,
medium-term, and generational consequences. Do not introduce additional miracles merely to make
the timeline work.

## Build the causal spine

Construct a private chain of four to seven links before publishing:

```text
divergence -> mechanism -> institutional response -> adaptation -> second-order effect
```

For every link, require a mechanism and a plausible delay. Preserve constraints such as physical
limits, human incentives, institutional inertia, path dependence, and uneven adoption. Permit
regional disagreement and failed adaptations. Do not let every consequence happen immediately or
benefit the same actors.

## Publish the editions

Produce at least three dated editions in chronological order. Each edition must contain:

- masthead, date, and place;
- one era-appropriate headline and deck;
- a concise news report showing a concrete event;
- one detail from ordinary life;
- one institutional, economic, cultural, or political consequence;
- a causal callback to an earlier edition.

Write each edition only with knowledge available at that point in the alternate timeline. Vary the
newsroom voice as norms and institutions evolve. Use fictional citizens or organizations when a
quote is useful; never invent quotations from real people.

Unless the user requests another length, use compact-edition mode: keep each edition's report
within roughly 180 Chinese characters or 120 English words, and keep the complete continuity desk
within roughly 500 Chinese characters or 300 English words. Preserve every required section by
cutting ornament rather than causal evidence.

## Run the continuity desk

After the editions, publish a compact continuity audit with:

| Earlier event | Mechanism | Later consequence | Continuity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

Rate each link `strong`, `plausible`, or `fragile`. Identify:

1. the weakest causal link;
2. one contradiction or missing transition, if any;
3. one alternative branch that could plausibly replace the weakest link;
4. one observable sign that would distinguish the two branches.

Revise an edition when its key event cannot be connected to the divergence without an unstated
miracle. Do not conceal uncertainty behind confident newspaper prose.

## Keep the reality boundary visible

Label the work as counterfactual. Separate real baseline facts from invented consequences. Do not
present fictional events, people, institutions, statistics, or quotations as real history.

Match the user's requested language. Prefer vivid specificity over excessive length.
